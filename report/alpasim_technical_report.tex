\begin{abstract}
This report presents GatedVLA, our entry for the Physical AI AV (PAI-AV) track. Our learned vision-language-action (VLA) policy pairs the driving-pretrained Qwen-Drive-1.0-4B vision-language model~\cite{qwendrive} with a layer-wise flow-matching action expert that outputs a 2.5\,s horizon of acceleration and curvature controls, augmented with a soft visual mask applied to the expert's cross-attention. Compared to an identically trained baseline, the masked policy achieves comparable overall open-loop error while reducing error on left turns. In local closed-loop evaluation across the same \RNScenes{} scenes (two rollouts per scene), the visual mask increases the mean scene score from \RMaskRef{} to \RMaskRun{} (paired difference $\RMaskDiff$, 95\% bootstrap CI $\RMaskCI$, wins/losses \RMaskWL, sign test $p=\RMaskP$). Incorporating a rule-based automatic emergency braking (AEB) module at deployment further increases the mean score to \RAebRun{} ($\RAebDiff$, CI $\RAebCI$) and reduces the mean number of scenes with at-fault collisions from \RMaskColl{} to \RAebColl{}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Even minor planning errors alter the subsequent observations encountered by a driving policy---a compounding effect that only closed-loop evaluation can reveal. The AlpaSim challenge evaluates policies in closed loop on held-out scenes~\cite{alpasim}, where a scene receives a score of zero if the ego vehicle causes an at-fault collision, drives off-road, or exits the recorded driving corridor. In our preliminary experiments, at-fault collisions were the primary source of score loss, predominantly involving slower or stationary lead vehicles when the planned trajectory failed to initiate braking. We therefore introduce (1) a learned visual mask trained to selectively retain image evidence relevant to navigation intent for the action expert, and (2) a detector-based emergency braking module that intervenes when the learned policy fails to decelerate.

\section{Related work}
\textbf{Vision-language-action driving.} Qwen-Drive-1.0~\cite{qwendrive} establishes a vision-language foundation model for autonomous driving paired with a planning expert. Alpamayo-R1~\cite{alpamayo} couples a reasoning model with a diffusion-based trajectory decoder, while DriveMoE~\cite{drivemoe} introduces expert routing to Drive-$\pi_0$, an adaptation of $\pi_0$~\cite{pi0}. We train a $\pi_0$-style flow-matching expert on top of the Qwen-Drive backbone.
\textbf{Limiting visual evidence.} S2~\cite{seeless} learns per-token soft masks under a visual evidence budget alongside an ungated pathway. We adopt a similar gating head and budget formulation, but apply the mask directly to the action expert's cross-attention logits, enforce an exact budget by construction, and train the mask via self-distillation (Section~\ref{sec:policy}).
\textbf{Copycat behavior.} Imitation learning policies can suffer from causal confusion by over-relying on their own past motion~\cite{causal}; ChauffeurNet~\cite{chauffeurnet} mitigates this with history dropout during training. We explored both history dropout and temporal ensembling~\cite{act}, but observed no clear gains (Appendix~\ref{app:others}).

\section{Method summary}
Our driving system (Fig.~\ref{fig:system}) comprises an end-to-end learned policy that replans every 0.5\,s and a detector-based AEB module that exclusively decelerates the planned trajectory along its predicted path. We evaluated and submitted three deployment variants derived from a single trained checkpoint: AEB at 10\,Hz, AEB at 5\,Hz, and the policy without AEB. Our implementation builds upon starVLA~\cite{starvla} and py123d\_garage~\cite{garage,py123d}, and all evaluations are conducted in AlpaSim~\cite{alpasim}.

\section{Policy description}
\label{sec:policy}
\textbf{Inputs.} Two front wide-angle camera frames (at $t_0$ and $t_0{-}0.1$\,s) along with the fixed text prompt ``predict trajectory'' are fed into the VLM. Ego-state history and navigation conditioning are provided exclusively to the action expert: five 10\,Hz historical steps of $[x, y, \mathrm{heading}, \mathrm{speed}, a_\mathrm{lon}]$ concatenated at each step with a target point $(x_\mathrm{tp}, y_\mathrm{tp})$ and a binary target-presence flag. During training, the target point is the first future ego position at a Euclidean distance of at least 25\,m; at deployment, it is the first such point along AlpaSim's map route (Appendix~\ref{app:driver}).

\textbf{Expert and outputs.} The action expert follows a $\pi_0$-style layer-wise architecture~\cite{pi0,drivemoe}: a 32-block Diffusion Transformer (DiT)~\cite{dit} alternates self-attention and cross-attention layers, where each cross-attention block attends to a corresponding VLM layer via a learned linear projection. The expert is trained via conditional flow matching~\cite{flowmatching} with a velocity MSE objective and generates trajectories using 10 Euler integration steps at inference. It predicts 25 steps of $[a_\mathrm{lon}, \mathrm{curvature}]$ controls at 10\,Hz, utilizing the kinematic unicycle action space adapted from NVIDIA Alpamayo~1.5~\cite{alpamayocode,alpamayo}. Integrating these controls from the current ego speed yields the output poses.

\textbf{Baseline.} The baseline shares the same architecture and training protocol but omits the visual mask and Paths B and C (below); as in Path A, the target point is dropped with probability $p=0.5$ per sample.

\textbf{Visual mask.} A gating module takes each visual token's detached final-layer VLM hidden state $h_p$ alongside a conditioning query $q$ (which embeds the scaled target point and ego state) and computes a logit score from $[h_p, q, h_p\odot q]$, following the visual evidence budget formulation of S2~\cite{seeless}. The scores are standardized across tokens within each frame to obtain $z_p$, yielding soft mask values $m_p=\sigma(b+2z_p)$, where the scalar bias $b$ is solved via bisection such that the per-frame mean of $m_p$ equals the target budget $\rho=0.2$ by construction, eliminating the need for an auxiliary budget loss. We add $\log m_p$ directly to the expert's cross-attention logits while leaving text tokens and internal VLM representations unmasked. Each training step executes three expert passes sharing a single VLM forward pass, sampled noise, and flow time step:
\vspace{-5pt}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}\setlength\parskip{0pt}\setlength\parsep{0pt}
\item \textbf{Path A} (ungated, target point dropped with $p=0.5$): minimizes the standard flow-matching loss and updates both the VLM and DiT.
\item \textbf{Path B} (stop-gradient mask, target point provided): minimizes $\lVert v_B-\mathrm{sg}(v_A)\rVert^2$ to update the DiT; this serves as the inference pathway.
\item \textbf{Path C} (active mask, target point omitted from the DiT state but provided to the gate): minimizes $\lVert v_C-\mathrm{sg}(v_A)\rVert^2$ and updates only the gating module.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{-5pt}
Paths B and C are distilled only on samples where the target point is retained in Path A; consequently, Path C forces the gating module to preserve visual tokens that encode the navigation intent.

\textbf{Emergency brake.} An RT-DETRv2-R18 detector~\cite{rtdetrv2,rtdetr} pretrained on COCO~\cite{coco} processes the current front-camera frame and retains boxes of seven traffic classes with confidence scores exceeding 0.5. The central 40\% of each bounding box's bottom edge is projected onto the ego rig's flat ground plane using the nominal $f$-$\theta$ camera calibration. Among detected obstacles within a 1.3\,m lateral corridor of the planned path, the closest obstacle defines the arc-length gap $d$ measured from the front bumper along the path; the closing speed $c$ is estimated via linear regression over the last 1\,s of $d$ (Table~\ref{tab:app_deploy}). With braking margin $g$ and required deceleration $a_\mathrm{req}$,
\begin{equation}
g = d - 2.5\,\mathrm{m} - 0.4\,\mathrm{s}\cdot c, \qquad a_\mathrm{req} = \frac{c^2}{2\max(g,\,0.1\,\mathrm{m})},
\end{equation}
the planned speed profile is capped by a linear deceleration ramp from the current speed $v$ down to the estimated lead speed $\max(v-c,0)$ at a rate of $\mathrm{clip}(1.2\,a_\mathrm{req},3,8)$\,m/s$^2$ whenever $g\le 0$ or $a_\mathrm{req}>3$\,m/s$^2$. Under a milder time-to-collision condition ($d/c<3$\,s), the planned speed is constrained not to exceed the current ego speed. Because the trajectory is re-timed along its own path from the ego vehicle's projection, a braked ego will not accelerate to catch up with a stale plan. Upon AEB release, the speed is held for one control tick before an immediate policy replan.

\section{Resources and training}
\textbf{Data.} Models are trained on the NVIDIA PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles dataset~\cite{paiav} (NVIDIA Autonomous Vehicle Dataset License Agreement), using only the front wide-angle camera streams and egomotion trajectories from the training split. No LiDAR, radar, or machine-generated perception labels were used.

\textbf{Pretrained weights.} We initialize the VLM from Qwen-Drive-1.0-4B~\cite{qwendrive} (Apache-2.0) and use off-the-shelf RT-DETRv2-R18 weights (\texttt{PekingU/rtdetr\_v2\_r18vd}, Apache-2.0) for detection. \textbf{Private resources:} None.

\textbf{Training.} Both the baseline (W\&B run \texttt{ccb412c9}) and masked policy (\texttt{03bdfc50}) were trained from the same code commit with random seed 42 for 12 epochs (38{,}400 steps) across 12 Intel Aurora XPU tiles. In the spirit of linear probing then fine-tuning (LP-FT)~\cite{lpft}, the VLM backbone remained frozen during the first epoch. Full hyperparameter settings and training curves are provided in the appendix.

\section{Runtime and reproducibility}
\textbf{Serving.} The submission container image is self-contained, operates within the challenge sandbox without external network access, and launches without arguments. Between 0.5\,s replans, it serves poses from the cached plan at 10\,Hz.

\textbf{Latency and memory.} The mean \texttt{Drive} latencies reported in Table~\ref{tab:closedloop} were recorded locally with driver video recording enabled and up to 12 concurrent rollouts sharing a single driver on multi-tenant hosts, so they are only indicative. In a standalone run with a single rollout per replica and video recording disabled (on a shared NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPU), the mean \texttt{Drive} RPC latency was 0.10--0.11\,s for both AEB variants (Appendix~\ref{app:runtime}). The RT-DETRv2 detector requires approximately 0.2\,GiB of VRAM in addition to the policy's 11.6\,GiB footprint.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The deployed inference configuration matches our logged W\&B training runs. Source code: \url{https://github.com/Azuma413/GatedVLA} (branch \texttt{main}, commit \texttt{80ab247}).

\section{Limitations and safety considerations}
\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Limited generalization evidence for AEB.} The AEB decision thresholds were tuned offline by replaying cached detections on the same 150 scenes evaluated in Table~\ref{tab:closedloop}, so the observed gains may be optimistic for held-out private test scenes.

\textbf{Remaining collisions.} Of the 12 remaining at-fault collisions in the first 10\,Hz AEB rollout (manual inspection, Appendix~\ref{app:closed}), 6 involved obstacles offset by approximately 2\,m laterally that entered the 1.3\,m detection corridor too late or not at all, a trade-off from narrowing the corridor to suppress false-positive braking; the rest involved agents from the side or rear-left (2), low-speed contacts where distance estimates stagnated (2), or braking too late or not to a complete stop (2).

\textbf{Side effects of emergency braking.} In the first rollout set, AEB increases the number of rear-ended scenes from 15 to 25. While these are not at-fault in the simulator, abrupt braking poses a genuine safety hazard in real-world traffic; moreover, 3 previously full-score scenes incurred new at-fault collisions under AEB.

\textbf{Dominance of lateral failures.} In the first AEB rollout, off-road departures and lateral corridor exits account for 0.253 of the 0.393 total score loss---failure modes that longitudinal AEB cannot mitigate. Off-road violations occurred at a median deviation of only 0.67\,m from the map route (comparable to normal deviations in successful rollouts), so we opted against deploying a route-distance guard.

\textbf{Suspected reliance on ego motion.} Without AEB, first-incident front collisions occur in \RMaskFrontRate\% of masked policy rollouts (\RMaskFrontN) vs.\ \RBaseFrontRate\% for the baseline (\RBaseFrontN). The visual mask appears to reduce this failure mode, but the final plan commanded no deceleration in \RMaskNoSlow\% vs.\ \RBaseNoSlow\% of these collisions (Table~\ref{tab:app_kin}).
